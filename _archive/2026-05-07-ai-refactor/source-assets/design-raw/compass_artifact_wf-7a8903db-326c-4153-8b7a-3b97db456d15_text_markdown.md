# Premium Website Design References for Platinental — A Curated Reference Library

The brief is unusually specific: build a singular brand experience for a premium plastic surgery and cosmetology clinic that reads more like *Vogue* or Aesop than like a medical practice. The references below were chosen because they share at least one of three properties Platinental's prototype currently lacks: (1) editorial typographic confidence, (2) aggressive use of negative space as an active design element, or (3) asymmetric/non-card layout systems that vary visual weight across the page. Each reference is annotated with what to steal, what to leave behind, and a 1–5 applicability rating against the locked brand identity ("intelligent harmonization of appearance without excess"; cream/ink palette; no clinical-medical iconography; no stock smiles).

## SECTION 2 — Luxury Beauty & Fashion Brand References

### 13. Aesop — aesop.com
- **(a)** Australian skin-care brand, brand-defining minimalism. The site uses Suisse Int'l and Zapf Humanist (Bitstream's Optima); Optima is also the logotype face. [Source](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/20234/aesop-logo-website-and-packaging)

## SECTION 3 — Layout Patterns & Cormorant-at-Scale References

### 20. Hôtel Weekend (Cormorant Garamond + Helvetica + Louize)
- **(a)** Listed in Maxibestof's font-pairing index as a documented site that uses Cormorant Garamond at display scale paired with Helvetica utility. [Source](https://maxibestof.one/typefaces/cormorant-garamond)

### 21. Polène — polene-paris.com (Canela + Cormorant + Sackers Gothic)

## DESIGN DIRECTION SYNTHESIS

### Where the prototype is right
The prototype's instincts — dark ink hero, oversized italic Cormorant, animated italic quote bridge — are tonally correct. The problem the brief identifies is real: every section is currently weighted equally, with the same vertical rhythm and the same level of decoration, so the page reads as a competent template rather than a singular brand. The fix is not to add more design but to redistribute weight: some sections should dominate, others should disappear; some should be photograph-led (when imagery exists), others should be entirely typographic; some should run edge-to-edge, others should sit within a narrow editorial column with vast empty margins. The unifying reference is *Lanserhof × Aesop × Augustinus Bader* — a longevity clinic's architectural calm, a beauty house's text-only confidence, a science-led skincare brand's deliberate asymmetry. Cormorant italic stays. Cream and ink stay. The structural grammar changes.

### Hero
Break the centered axis. The H1 should sit in the upper-left two-thirds of the viewport, set in italic Cormorant at roughly twice the current visual weight, with line-leading tight enough that the lines almost touch — the italic legs of one line should nearly graze the ascenders of the next, which is the move that turns Cormorant from "pretty" to "editorial". Indent the H1 from the left edge by approximately the width of one of its own characters (call it a one-em indent) so it never tightropes the viewport. The supporting paragraph and primary CTA cluster should anchor the lower-right quadrant, set in the utility grotesque at body size, separated from the H1 by an empty middle band that does no design work — that empty band is the signal of premium. The before/after slider, currently inside the hero, should drop below the type as a second beat with its own breathing room, not behind it as a competing photographic surface. The bottom stats strip should also move down: stats live with the slider, not with the headline. The ratio target is roughly two parts type to one part empty space to one part image — the inverse of most clinic sites.

### Service & Procedure Sections
Replace the three equal cards entirely. The three service directions (surgery, cosmetology, cosmeceuticals) should become three full-viewport editorial spreads, each numbered (01 / 02 / 03) with a small uppercase utility label pinned left. Each spread carries one display-scale italic Cormorant phrase (the direction's name framed editorially — not "Plastic Surgery" but something like "*The architecture of the face*"), a single paragraph of brand-voice prose, and a hairline-ruled index of the relevant procedures. The 27 surgical operations should never appear as cards; they appear as a long indexed list — procedure name set in a slightly heavier serif weight, one-line positioning in body grotesque, recovery-time or duration in a small caps tabular figure, separated by hairline rules. Hover changes type weight only; nothing animates, nothing slides. This is the Aesop / Sisley move and it's the single highest-leverage change in the redesign.

### Quote Bridge
Keep the dark inverted section, but the giant italic quote should be allowed to break out of the column grid and sit in true full-bleed type — leading edge of the first letter touching (or even overshooting) the left viewport edge, last letter doing the same on the right. The animation should be confined to micro-movement of italic stress (a slow hover-tilt, or a scroll-tied subtle slant shift) rather than reveal animation. This is the Aman / The Row move — slowness as a tonal signature.

### Doctors Section
Without portrait photography, the only credible move is to lean fully typographic and refuse the card grid. Each doctor becomes an editorial half-spread: large italic Cormorant set initials or full name as a typographic monogram (not a circular avatar), a single representative quote in italic at sub-display scale, and credentials surfaced as marginalia in the utility face — degrees, years, surgical specializations. Treat doctors as authors, not products. Sturm's About-Dr-Sturm essay is the structural model. When professional photography eventually arrives, the layout already has slots for it: the typographic monogram becomes the photo's caption frame, and the existing editorial slots fill with portrait. The design should be photograph-ready but not photograph-dependent.

### Typography Scale & Spacing Rules
Three faces, no more: italic Cormorant Garamond (or Editorial New italic, if the budget allows for Cyrillic-grade rendering) for display and quotation only; a neutral humanist or geometric grotesque for body, navigation, and form chrome; a small uppercase utility cut (which can be the same grotesque at heavy small caps with wide tracking) for eyebrow labels, numbers, and procedure-list utility. The hierarchy should compress — the prototype likely has too many intermediate sizes. Aim for: one display size (hero and section openers, all italic, all generous), one sub-display (procedure-list names, roman or italic, smaller but still confidently larger than body), one body size, one small-caps utility size. Line-height tightens dramatically as size grows: display type sets nearly solid (lines touching), body sets generous. Tracking inverts: display type tracks tight or even slightly negative; small-caps utility tracks wide. Every section should have a left margin equal to roughly one-fifth of the viewport width — never edge-to-edge content except in the deliberate full-bleed quote moments. Whitespace is the brand; treat empty vertical space between sections as a designed element with its own size hierarchy (a "small gap," "medium gap," and "deep breath" between major direction transitions).

### What to refuse
No before/after grids beyond the single hero slider. No equal-weight three-card grids anywhere. No icon system for procedures. No medical-blue or red accents. No saturated lifestyle photography. No CTA buttons treated as colored rectangles — buttons should be underlined italic links with hover-only chrome, or a single thin-bordered pill in ink, never filled. No rounded corners larger than hairline. No drop shadows. No carousels except the deliberate before/after slider. The discipline of subtraction is what gets Platinental from "polished template" to "singular brand."
